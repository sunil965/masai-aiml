The Situation

An ice cream shop wants to predict daily sales from the day's temperature.

Sales generally rise as it gets warmer — but on the very hottest days, people sometimes stay indoors instead of walking to the shop, so the true relationship gently curves rather than following a straight line. You have to pick how complex a model to fit to this pattern. Too simple, and it misses the curve entirely. Too complex, and it starts fitting the random day-to-day noise instead of the real trend.

Both mistakes have names — bias and variance — and they trade off against each other as a model gets more complex.

Two Different Ways to Be Wrong

Bias vs. Variance

High Bias

The model is too simple to capture the real pattern — a straight line drawn through a curve. It's wrong in a systematic way, and wrong on new data for the same reason it's wrong on training data. This is underfitting.

High Variance

The model is so flexible it bends to chase every noisy data point instead of the underlying trend. It looks excellent on the data it trained on, and falls apart on anything new. This is overfitting.

The Trade-off

As a model gets more complex, bias falls — but variance rises

Training error Validation error

Training error keeps falling as the model gets more flexible — it can always fit its own training data better. Validation error falls, then rises again: past some point, extra complexity is only fitting noise. The dip is the sweet spot.

Worked Example

Three models, same 60 days of sales data

Degree 1 (straight line)104.784.8High bias
Degree 2 (gentle curve)84.159.9Good balance
Degree 15 (highly flexible)68.8245.2High variance

The degree-15 model has the lowest training error of the three — it's memorized the training days almost perfectly — but by far the highest test error. That gap between a great training score and a poor test score is the signature of high variance. The degree-2 model gives up a little training accuracy in exchange for the best result on unseen days.

Diagnosing Underfitting and Overfitting

Read the gap between training and validation error

HIGH BIAS · UNDERFITTING

Both errors are high, and close together

The model can't even fit the data it trained on well — it's too simple for the pattern, on any data.

HIGH VARIANCE · OVERFITTING

Training error is low, validation error is much higher

A large gap between the two is the tell — the model learned the training set specifically, not the general pattern.

Fixes differ accordingly: high bias calls for a more flexible model or better features; high variance calls for more training data, regularization, or a simpler model.
